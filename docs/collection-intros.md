# Collection intros

Spec section 6.3. Every collection gets 60 to 100 words above the grid, written to
orient someone who landed there rather than to feed a search engine.

These go in the **collection description** in Shopify admin, not in the theme. The
collection template already renders the title and description above the product
grid, so pasting the text into the description is the whole job.

## Parent collections

### Food packaging

Everything the food goes home in. Containers, clamshells, deli cups, foil pans,
bags, wrap. Sizes overlap on purpose, because a kitchen that runs one container
line usually needs two or three sizes of it and wants them to stack. If you are
switching suppliers, start here and check the per-piece price against your last
invoice. It is the category where the difference between two suppliers shows up
fastest, since you buy it every week.

### Food service

What goes on the table and in the customer's hand. Cups and lids, cutlery, plates
and bowls, straws, trays, carriers. Hot and cold cups are listed separately
because the lids are not interchangeable and ordering the wrong pair is the most
common mistake in this category. Cups and lids are sold separately unless the
product title says sets, so check the matching lid before you check out.

### Disposable gloves

Nitrile, vinyl, latex, poly and heavy industrial, in sizes S through XL. The
material matters more than the price per box. Nitrile costs more and lasts longer
under oil and citrus, vinyl is cheapest for short handling tasks, poly is for
sandwich stations where a glove is changed every minute. Sizes are variants, so
pick the size on the product page rather than hunting for a separate listing.

### Paper products

Towels, napkins, bath tissue and seat covers. Bulky, low value per case, and the
category where freight quietly eats the saving if you buy it three cases at a
time. This is the clearest argument for consolidating an order: paper is what
pushes a cart over a tier and over the free shipping line without pushing the
budget much.

### Janitorial and cleaning

Chemicals, trash liners and can liners. Liners are sorted by gauge and by can
size rather than by brand name, because the gauge is what decides whether a bag
holds a wet load. If you are not sure which gauge you are on now, check the box
you have open. It is printed on the flap more often than not.

## Food packaging sub-collections

### Take-out containers

The working set for delivery and pickup. Kraft fold-top boxes, plastic hinged
containers, round bowls, rectangular bases. What separates them in practice is
whether the lid seats with a snap or a friction fit, since a friction fit fails
when the bag goes on its side in a car. Sizes run small enough for a side and
large enough for a family portion.

### Microwaveable containers

Polypropylene bases with snap lids, rated to go from the freezer to the microwave
without warping or cracking. That pair of problems is what kills cheaper bowls,
and it is worth paying a few cents a piece to avoid. Standard choice for rice
bowls, curries, poke and anything with sauce in it. Base and lid ship together as
sets on most items in this collection, and the product page says which.

### Deli and soup containers

Round containers from 8 oz to 64 oz, with one lid that fits several sizes in most
lines. Cups and lids are ordered separately. A buyer who orders 500 containers
and no lids finds out on a Friday, so the product page links the matching lid
where the pairing is set. Good for soup, sauces, dressings, prepped mise and cold
grab-and-go.

### Hinged clamshells

Four different closure types, and they behave differently. Corner lock holds
tightest and is the safest bet for delivery. Centre slide is quicker to close
one-handed at a busy pass. Duo front tabs vent steam, which keeps fries from
going soft but lets heat out faster. Two-colour and translucent are the same
build with a clear lid, for when the food should sell itself. Sizes run 6" to
11", one and three compartment.

### Paper food containers and pails

Kraft and white paper pails, cups and folded boxes for hot food. Paper handles
heat well and stacks flat in storage, which is the reason a small kitchen with no
shelf space keeps coming back to it. It does not hold a wet load as long as
plastic, so it suits food that goes out and gets eaten rather than food that sits
in a fridge overnight.

### Aluminium pans and foil lids

Steam table pans, round pans, loaf and pie sizes, with board or dome lids sold
separately. Foil goes in the oven and holds heat on a catering line better than
anything else in the catalog. Check the lid line before ordering, since dome lids
and board lids fit different pan rims. TI/HI is listed on each product for anyone
buying by the pallet.

### Carry-out bags

T-shirt bags, handled paper bags and flat kraft bags. The two numbers that matter
are the gauge on plastic and the basis weight on paper, because both decide
whether the bag holds a full order without a second bag. Sizes are listed by
width, gusset and height in that order, which is how the bags are measured in the
trade.

### Food wrap and deli paper

Cling film, foil sheets, deli sheets and wrap paper. Sold by the roll or the
case. The interleaved sheets pull faster at a sandwich station than a roll does,
which is worth more than the price difference once a line gets busy. Sizes are
listed by sheet dimension.

### Stretch and pallet wrap

Hand wrap and machine wrap by gauge and roll width. Buy the gauge for the load
rather than the lowest price per roll, since a thin wrap gets used twice as fast
on a heavy pallet and costs more per pallet in the end.

## Food service sub-collections

### Hot cups and lids

Single and double wall paper cups, 4 oz through 20 oz, with lids sold separately.
Double wall holds heat longer and stays comfortable in the hand without a
sleeve, which is worth the difference for a cafe selling drip coffee to go.
Check the lid line matches the cup line before ordering. The two are not
interchangeable across sizes even when the rim looks the same.

### Cold cups and lids

PET and PP cups with flat, dome and strawless lids. PET is clear and rigid and
shows the drink well, which is why it is standard for iced coffee and cold brew.
PP is softer, cheaper and holds up better to a knock. Lids are ordered
separately and are matched by rim diameter rather than by capacity.

### Portion cups and lids

Seven sizes from 0.75 oz to 5.5 oz, with three lids covering all of them. PC075
and PC100 share a lid. PC150 and PC200 share a lid. PC325, PC400 and PC550 share
a lid. Cups and lids are sold separately, 2,500 to a case, so check you are
ordering both.

### Cup carriers and accessories

Board carriers, drink trays and sleeves. The item most often forgotten until a
delivery driver spills an order, and the cheapest insurance in the catalog
against a refund. Carriers are sized by cup count rather than by cup volume.

### Cutlery

Wrapped and unwrapped, PP and heavier weight, plus compostable lines. Weight is
the thing to check. A light fork bends on anything denser than salad, and a
customer who bends a fork on the first bite remembers it. Wrapped sets cost more
per piece and save labour at a counter that assembles bags by hand.

### Plates and bowls

Paper, foam, plastic and bagasse, in the sizes a kitchen actually plates on.
Bagasse handles hot food and cuts cleanly under a knife. Foam is the cheapest per
piece and is restricted in a growing number of cities, so check your local rule
before standardising on it.

### Straws

Paper, PLA and plastic, wrapped and unwrapped, in regular, jumbo and boba
diameters. Paper straws vary a lot in how long they hold up in a cold drink,
which is the one thing customers complain about. Wrapped is required for a
grab-and-go counter in most health codes.

### Food and deli trays

Foam and board trays for meat, deli and grab-and-go cases. Sized by the standard
tray numbering, so a 2S here is the same footprint as the 2S you already use.
Good for a case that sells by sight, since the tray sets how the food sits.

## Glove sub-collections

### Nitrile gloves

Nitrile costs more than vinyl and outlasts it by enough to be cheaper per hour of
work. It does not tear at the thumb web the way vinyl does, and it holds up to
citrus and oil. Black is standard front of house because stains do not show.
Sizes S through XL, 4 mil, powder free, food-contact compliant. Order one size up
from what people say they wear, because nobody ever does.

### Vinyl gloves

The cheapest glove that is still food-contact compliant, and the right choice for
short tasks where a glove gets changed every few minutes anyway. It tears sooner
than nitrile under oil and at the thumb web, so it is a poor fit for prep that
runs for an hour. Powder free, sizes S through XL.

### Latex gloves

Better fit and feel than vinyl at a similar price, with the grip most cooks
prefer. The reason it is not the default any more is allergy. If anyone on your
line or in your dining room reacts to latex, buy nitrile instead and do not think
about it again.

### Poly, PE and TPE gloves

Loose-fitting, single-use, and cheap enough to change between every sandwich.
Built for speed at a counter rather than for dexterity or durability. TPE is the
stretchier version and stays on the hand better than plain poly.

### Industrial gloves

Heavy gauge nitrile and lined gloves for dish pits, cleaning and anything caustic.
Thicker than a food-prep glove, longer at the cuff, and reusable through a shift.
Check the gauge rather than the size when comparing two of these.

## Paper sub-collections

### Paper towels

Roll towels, multifold and centre pull, plus the dispensers they fit. Fold type
is decided by the dispenser you already have on the wall, so check that before
switching. Centre pull uses less per hand dry than multifold in most kitchens,
which shows up over a year rather than over a week.

### Napkins

Beverage, luncheon and dinner sizes, one and two ply. Dispenser napkins run at
about half the usage of a stacked napkin caddy, because people take one at a
time. Sizes are listed folded, which is how they arrive.

### Bath tissue

Standard roll, jumbo roll and coreless, sorted by dispenser type. Jumbo roll costs
less per sheet and needs the matching dispenser. Check the roll diameter against
the housing before ordering a case.

### Toilet seat covers

Half-fold covers and the dispensers for them. Required in a public restroom in
most California jurisdictions, and the sort of thing that gets noticed on an
inspection rather than by a customer.

## Janitorial sub-collections

### Cleaning chemicals

Degreasers, sanitisers, floor and glass cleaners, in concentrate and ready to
use. Concentrate costs less per use and needs a dilution station and a trained
hand. `[SDS SHEETS AND ANY CALIFORNIA-SPECIFIC HANDLING NOTES GO HERE ONCE
SUPPLIER DOCS ARE IN HAND]`

### Trash bags and can liners

Sorted by can size and by gauge, since those two decide whether a bag holds a wet
load without splitting on the way to the dumpster. Low density stretches around
sharp waste and high density resists tearing on smooth loads. Buying a gauge down
to save a few dollars a case is the most common false economy in this category.

## Shop by business

### Cloud kitchens

Packaging that survives the drive and still looks right at the door. Vented
clamshells that hold heat without steaming the food soft, containers whose lids
seat with a full-perimeter snap rather than a friction fit, and bags that hold a
full order without doubling. If you run several brands out of one space,
standardising on one container line across all of them is usually worth more than
the price difference between two.

### Caterers

Buying in bursts rather than weekly, which makes the cart-wide tier count worth
real money on a single event order. Foil pans and board lids for holding, sturdy
plates that do not flex under a full plate, and cutlery heavy enough to eat with.
Keep the sizes consistent across a season so a job quoted in March can be
delivered in August at the same cost per head.

### Food trucks

Small storage, tight margins and no room for the wrong order. The case sizes here
are the ones that fit under a counter, and the products are the ones that do two
jobs rather than one. Ordering a month at a time and hitting a tier beats
ordering weekly at standard price, if you can find the shelf for it.

### Cafes and coffee shops

Hot cups, cold cups and lids without the per-cup markup a distributor puts on
them. Double wall for anything served over 70 degrees, PET for cold drinks that
sell on how they look, and lids matched by rim diameter rather than by capacity.
Cup usage is predictable enough that this is the easiest category to buy up a
tier on.

### Delis and supermarkets

Grab-and-go packaging that sells the food inside it. Clear lids for the case,
deli containers with lids that fit several sizes, trays sized to the standard
numbering you already use, and film that seals rather than slides. Label
placement matters as much as the container, so check the flat area on the lid
before switching lines.

### Full-service restaurants

One supplier for the whole back of house. Take-out packaging for the delivery
side, gloves and paper for the line, chemicals and liners for close. The cart-wide
tier count is built for exactly this order shape, since a restaurant that buys
all four categories at once reaches a tier that none of the four would reach
alone.
